# The Secret World of Scams

## Overview
This project explores how scams have evolved from confidence tricks and mail fraud to modern phishing, romance scams, deepfakes, and AI-generated fraud. It was created as part of UNSW COMP6441, with the goal of breaking down the tactics scammers use so people can more confidently spot and avoid them.

## Contents
- **Evolution of Scams** — tracing scams from the earliest recorded case in 300 BC through the Ponzi scheme, early digital scams (e.g. the Nigerian Prince email), the rise of cryptocurrency fraud, pandemic-era scams, and the emergence of AI-driven scams
- **Types of Scams** — phishing (email, SMS, fake login pages), investment scams, romance scams, tech/social media scams, AI-generated deepfakes, ransomware, and in-person scams
- **Why We Are Susceptible** — the psychological and social factors that make people vulnerable, including emotional manipulation, cognitive bias, social influence, and lack of awareness
- **Anatomy of a Scam** — a step-by-step breakdown of how attackers plan and execute phishing, romance, and fake payment scams, from choosing a target to monetising stolen information
- **Effects of Scams** — the impact on victims, common target archetypes, and the often-overlooked environmental cost of the infrastructure (data centres) that powers scam operations
- **How to Stay Protected** — practical guidance for spotting scams, including the AUSGUT framework (Asking for info, dodgy URL, Sense of urgency, strange Greeting, Unusual payment request, suspicious email address Text) and tips for identifying AI-generated deepfakes
- **Simulation** — a hands-on demonstration used to illustrate scam tactics in practice

## Notable Case Studies
- The Ponzi Scheme
- The Spanish Prisoner Scam
- WannaCry Ransomware
- AI deepfake video call fraud (2023 Hong Kong incident)

## Key Takeaway
Technology and detection tools will keep evolving, but so will scam tactics — awareness and healthy scepticism remain the most reliable defence.
